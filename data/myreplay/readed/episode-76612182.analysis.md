## Good Pattern

p2's winning sequence is not an opening advantage. It is a late 4P snowball:

- At step `340`, p2 has become the production leader: `17` production versus
  p1's `16`, p3's `10`, and p0's `9`.
- From steps `368-481`, p2 keeps converting contested/high-production planets:
  - step `368`: captures p0's production `4` planet `6`.
  - step `384`: captures p3's production `3` planet `22`.
  - step `407`: captures p3's production `3` planet `23`.
  - step `436`: captures p3's production `3` planet `21`.
  - step `467`: captures p1's production `3` planet `20`.
  - step `481`: captures p1's production `4` planet `4`.
- Once p0 collapses at step `400`, p2 has enough production and ships to win
  the remaining side fights.

This is the positive side of aggressive 4P play: once a neighbor collapses, the
agent can exploit the exposed production and finish the map.

## Bad Pattern

The losing players do not coordinate against the emerging leader:

- p0 collapses first, reaching `0` planets by step `400`.
- p1 briefly has `19` production at step `400`, but much of that is from eating
  p0 rather than attacking p2.
- p3 is still fighting local non-leader targets while p2 is already ahead.
- The current promoted 4P source penalty can reduce risky source overuse, but it
  does not add any pressure toward the current production leader.

Concrete failure family:

- Late 4P leader containment.
- Trigger window begins around step `320-360` when one player has a production
  lead and another player is collapsing.
- The desired behavior is not a hard attack rule. It should be a small target
  score bonus against leader-owned planets, preserving local-distance and ROI
  filters.
