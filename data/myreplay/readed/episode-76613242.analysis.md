## Good Pattern

p0 wins by combining faster opening expansion with immediate punishment:

- p0 captures the first prod3 neutral at step `36`.
- p1 captures a prod3 neutral at step `40`, four ticks later.
- p0 keeps chaining low-cost captures from step `51` onward and converts the
  ship lead into p1-owned planets:
  - step `74`: p0 captures p1 home planet `15`.
  - step `80`: p0 captures p1 planet `23`.
  - step `104`: p0 captures p1 planet `13`.
  - steps `120`, `123`, `125`: p0 captures p1 planets `19`, `17`, and prod3
    planet `6`.

The good behavior is not a single all-in. It is early tempo plus repeated
punishment of sources that have been drained.

## Bad Pattern

p1's failure has two parts:

- Opening tempo: p1's first meaningful attack is step `35`, while p0 launches
  at step `25`.
- Front-source overuse: after p1 owns prod3 planet `6`, it repeatedly launches
  from that source while already behind. The largest example is step `75` in
  the replay log: p1 sends `51` ships from planet `6`, plus smaller attacks
  from planets `19` and `23`.

The existing 2P source-threat experiments focused on production-4 sources. This
replay's critical source is production `3`, so those guards do not change the
observed step `72-84` decisions when replay-fed into current `regular`.

Concrete failure family:

- 2P mid-opening front-source overuse from production-3 planets.
- Trigger window is roughly steps `55-100`.
- Desired behavior is softer than the old prod4 2P source penalty: avoid
  draining a contested prod3 front source when nearby enemy production can
  recapture it, but do not broadly convert draws into losses.
